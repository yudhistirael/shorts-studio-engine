# Shorts Studio Engine

Use the creator's brief, or choose a narrow niche with repeatable topics, a clear audience, reliable sources, and visual storytelling opportunities. Set a per-episode budget and establish a topic ledger in the working project. Produce one episode when asked.

1. Choose a fresh topic and describe the audience promise in one line. Verify material claims and retain sources in the production notes.
2. Write a short script and shot list. Open on a real situation or visible question, then reveal the cause through close-ups, comparisons, or a reconstruction. End with a clear answer.
3. Choose an available animation engine. Use an existing scene or template, adapt its actual geometry and animation, and render a short representative shot before full production. Choose 2D, 3D, or a mix based on the story and budget. Use [production guidance](references/production.md).
4. Render the final vertical MP4 with audible narration, phrase subtitles, and a complete ending. Integrate the episode title in the first three seconds; export an actual frame with that title as a vertical thumbnail and record its timestamp.
5. Inspect actual video, audio, claims, captions, and mobile framing. Write a QA JSON tied to the final file's SHA-256. Report incomplete work as a preview.
6. Deliver the MP4, thumbnail, title, and short caption. Use the same caption for the YouTube description and TikTok. Store sources separately in the brief.
7. If the creator has authorized publishing to a specific connected channel, use [YouTube publishing](references/youtube.md). Otherwise deliver local files. Report a schedule only after remote verification.
